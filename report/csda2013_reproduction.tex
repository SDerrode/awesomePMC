\begin{abstract}
We reproduce the simulation experiments of sections~3.2, 3.3 and~4.3 of
Derrode and Pieczynski~\cite{derrode2013} with the open-source
\texttt{awesomePMC} package. Three findings shape the reproduction.
\emph{(i)}~The model of the paper is the general pairwise Markov chain whose
margins $f_{ij}$ are indexed by the \emph{pair} of states (Eqs.~12--14 of the
paper); earlier versions of the package collapsed them to one margin per
state, which by the Proposition of the paper's §2.1 turns the PMC into a
hidden Markov chain and put the diagonal error rates 7 to 19 points above the paper's on
average.
\emph{(ii)}~The second parameter of the Gaussian margins of the paper's
Table~1 is a variance: only that reading makes the printed Gamma parameters
consistent. \emph{(iii)}~The unsupervised experiment needs several ICE starts:
with Gaussian margins, a single start ends on a copula layout of markedly lower
likelihood.
With the general PMC, Tables~2(a), 2(b), 3(b) and~4(a) are reproduced to within
half a point on average over their 49 cells; with a multistart ICE, the
diagonal copulas of Tables~6--7 are recovered in 97--100\,\% of runs and the
unsupervised error comes within half a point of the supervised one. We document the gaps that
remain: Table~3(a) (Gaussian margins, $\tau=0.70$) and the PMM Tables~4(b)
and~5(b).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

The CSDA-2013 paper introduces an unsupervised classification method for
sequences modelled by a \emph{stationary and reversible pairwise Markov
chain} (SR-PMC) whose bivariate observation densities are built from
copulas. Three sets of experiments illustrate, in order:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item the influence of the copula \emph{shape} on supervised restoration,
        when the simulation copula and the restoration copula vary
        independently (paper~§3.2, Tables~2--3; our Section~\ref{sec:exp1});
  \item the same study with the i.i.d.\ \emph{pairwise mixture model} (PMM),
        which isolates the effect of Markovianity (paper~§3.3, Tables~4--5;
        Section~\ref{sec:exp2});
  \item the recovery of the copula families and parameters by ICE with
        automatic copula selection (paper~§4.3, Tables~6--7;
        Section~\ref{sec:exp3}).
\end{enumerate}
Section~\ref{sec:exp4} adds a comparison of six selection criteria that is
not in the paper. The radar-image segmentation of the paper's §5 is not
reproduced.

This report replaces an earlier version whose numbers were produced with one
margin per state and a single ICE start. Section~\ref{sec:setup} explains
why both choices were wrong and how the reproduction was corrected; the
earlier results are kept in the repository and are quoted where the
comparison is informative.

\section{Setup and notation}\label{sec:setup}

\subsection{The general PMC with copulas}\label{sec:model}

A PMC is a Markov chain $Z_n=(X_n,Y_n)$ with hidden states $X_n\in\{1,\dots,K\}$
and observations $Y_n$. In the stationary and reversible case considered by
the paper, its law is given by the joint probabilities $p_{ij}=p(x_n=i,
x_{n+1}=j)$, $p_{ij}=p_{ji}$, and by the density of a pair of consecutive
observations given the pair of states (paper, Eq.~12):
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pair}
  f(y_n,y_{n+1}\mid x_n=i,\,x_{n+1}=j)
   = f_{ij}(y_n)\,f_{ji}(y_{n+1})\,
     c_{ij}\bigl(F_{ij}(y_n),\,F_{ji}(y_{n+1})\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $f_{ij}$ is the law of $y_n$ given $(x_n,x_{n+1})=(i,j)$, $F_{ij}$ its
CDF and $c_{ij}$ a copula density. Reversibility only makes the right margin
of the pair $(i,j)$ the left margin of the pair $(j,i)$ --- the index
inversion $f_{ji}$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:pair}. The transitions follow
(paper, Eqs.~13--14):
\begin{gather}
  p(x_{n+1}=j\mid x_n=i,\,y_n)\;\propto\;p_{ij}\,f_{ij}(y_n),\\
  p(y_{n+1}\mid x_n=i,\,x_{n+1}=j,\,y_n)=f_{ji}(y_{n+1})\,c_{ij}\bigl(F_{ij}(y_n),\,F_{ji}(y_{n+1})\bigr).
\end{gather}

\paragraph{State margins make $X$ Markov.} The Proposition of the paper's
§2.1 states that, for an SR-PMC, $X$ is a Markov chain if and only if
$p(y_2\mid x_1,x_2)=p(y_2\mid x_2)$, i.e.\ if and only if $f_{ij}=f_i$ does not
depend on~$j$. Versions~0.5.0 to~0.8 of the package imposed $f_{ij}=f_i$,
attributing it to reversibility. The model called ``PMC'' was then a
stationary reversible HMC with correlated noise, not the model of the paper.
The package now keeps both structures, selected by
\texttt{[model].margin\_structure}: \texttt{"state"} ($K$ margins $f_i$) and
\texttt{"pair"} ($K^2$ margins $f_{ij}$, the general PMC).

\subsection{Margins of the paper's Table~1}\label{sec:margins}

Table~1 of the paper gives four margins for $K=2$: Gaussian
$\mathcal N(\mu,s)$ with $(\mu,s)=(0,1)$, $(0.3,1.6)$, $(1.1,1.4)$, $(1.5,1)$
for $f_{11},f_{12},f_{21},f_{22}$, and Gamma margins
$\mathcal G(\lambda,\alpha,\theta)$ ``chosen so that their standard deviations
are identical to those of Gaussian distributions''.

\paragraph{The second Gaussian parameter is a variance.} The printed Gamma
parameters are consistent with the caption only if $\lambda$ is the location
of the \emph{centred} Gamma variable (the mean $\mu$ being added afterwards),
$\theta$ is the square of the scale, and $s$ is a \emph{variance}. For a Gamma
margin of shape $\alpha$ and standard deviation~$\sigma$ these quantities are
$\lambda=-\sqrt\alpha\,\sigma$ and $\theta=\sigma^2/\alpha$.
Table~\ref{tab:table1} compares the printed values with the two readings of
$s$: reading $s$ as a variance reproduces $f_{11}$, $f_{21}$ and $f_{22}$ up
to the rounding of the printed values and $f_{12}$ to $0.12$ on $\lambda$, whereas reading it as a standard
deviation misses $f_{12}$ and $f_{21}$ entirely.

\subsection{Reading the paper's tables}\label{sec:orientation}

In Tables~2--5 of the paper, rows are the \emph{tested} (restoration) copula
and columns the \emph{true} (simulation) copula --- ``when the true copula is
$c^3$ and the tested copula is $c^5$, we get an error of $14.11\,\%$'' --- and
the minimum of each column, i.e.\ the best restoration copula for each true
copula, is highlighted. Our tables follow the same orientation. (The earlier
version of this report printed the transpose.)

\section{Reproducibility}\label{sec:reproducibility}

The results come from the script \nolinkurl{report/reproduce_csda2013.py} and
every table from \nolinkurl{report/csda2013_tables.py}, which reads the CSV
results and the values of the paper
(\nolinkurl{report/data/csda2013_tables2to5.csv}). The diagnostics of
Sections~\ref{sec:margins} and~\ref{sec:exp3-basin} come from the scripts
kept with the working notes (\nolinkurl{report/out/csda_compare/}, not
versioned). The runs
reported here are:
\begin{lstlisting}
# Experiments 1-2 (Tables 2-5): 30 replications per cell, N = 2000
python report/reproduce_csda2013.py --exp1 --exp2 --reps 30 --margins pair
# Experiment 3 (Tables 6-7), single ICE start: 100 runs
python report/reproduce_csda2013.py --exp3 --runs 100 --margins pair
# Experiment 3, multistart ICE: 30 runs
python report/reproduce_csda2013.py --exp3 --runs 30 --margins pair --exp3-starts multi
# Tables of this report
python report/csda2013_tables.py
\end{lstlisting}
\texttt{--margins pair} reads $s$ as a variance by default
(\texttt{--table1-sigma}); \texttt{--margins state} reproduces the earlier
one-margin-per-state results. The paper used 300 replications per
cell and 10 ICE runs. Every experiment uses explicit seeds. With five worker processes on a laptop,
Experiments~1--2 take a few minutes, the
single-start Experiment~3 about 40 minutes and the multistart one about
3.5~hours.

\section{Experiment~2 --- Pairwise mixture model\\
        \emph{(CSDA Tables~4 and~5)}}\label{sec:exp2}

\subsection{Setup}

In the PMM, the pairs $(y_1,y_2),(y_3,y_4),\dots$ are independent. We draw
$N/2=1000$ pairs, the first observation from $f_{ij}$ and the second from
$f_{ji}$ with copula $c_{ij}$, and classify each pair with the posterior
\begin{equation}
   p(x_{1}=i \mid y_{1},y_{2}) \propto
     \sum_{j=1}^{K} p_{ij}\,f_{ij}(y_1)\,f_{ji}(y_2)\,
     c_{ij}\bigl(F_{ij}(y_1),F_{ji}(y_2)\bigr).
\end{equation}
The (simulation, restoration) combinations are those of
Experiment~1.

\subsection{Discussion}

Markovianity lowers the error of the correct restoration (diagonal cells)
in every configuration. At $\tau=0.16$ the PMC error is $32$--$40\,\%$ lower
than the PMM one with Gaussian margins and $36$--$42\,\%$ lower with Gamma

Clayton copulas but only $6$--$21\,\%$ for $c^1$, $c^2$, $c^3$, $c^7$ and

$\tau=0.70$ come from the PMC side, where our errors exceed the paper's
(Table~3(a), Section~\ref{sec:agreement}).

\paragraph{What the Table~3(a) gap is not.} The excess is confined to the
PMC with Gaussian margins at $\tau=0.70$ and to copula-aware restorations:
restoring the same data with the product copula (row $c^0$) matches the
paper to within $1$--$1.7$~points, and the PMM of Table~5(a), which uses the
same pair density~\eqref{eq:pair} without the chain dynamics, matches to
$+1.1$~points on the diagonal. We tested seven explanations on the diagonal
cells (20--30 replications), requiring each to fix Table~3(a) without
breaking the tables that already agree:
\begin{itemize}
  \item reading $s$ as a standard deviation: Table~3(a) $+3.3$ on average,
        Table~2(a) degraded to $-1.1$;
  \item exchanging $f_{12}$ and $f_{21}$, with either reading of $s$:
        Table~3(a) $-0.8$ and $-0.7$, but Tables~2(a), 2(b) and~3(b) move
        by $-2.7$ to $-5.2$ points;
  \item simulating $X$ as a Markov chain (Eq.~13 ignoring $y_n$): Table~3(a)
        $+9.1$;
  \item Eq.~13 evaluated with $f_{ji}(y_n)$ instead of $f_{ij}(y_n)$:
        Table~3(a) $-0.3$, but Tables~2(a), 2(b) and~3(b) move to $-2.5$,
        $-2.7$ and $-3.7$, and the product-copula cell to $9.4$ against
        $11.06\,\%$;
  \item reading $s$ as a variance in Eq.~13 and as a standard deviation in
        Eq.~14 and the copula arguments, or the reverse: Table~3(a) $+3.7$
        and $+3.4$.
\end{itemize}
Three independent implementations of the exact model --- the package, a
stand-alone simulator and a separately written forward--backward --- give the
same error rates. The gap is therefore not a coding error in the model as
the paper states it; the 2013 computation of Table~3(a) presumably differed
in a way the paper does not describe. It remains open.

\section{Experiment~3 --- Unsupervised copula selection by ICE\\
        \emph{(CSDA Tables~6 and~7)}}\label{sec:exp3}

\subsection{Setup}

$N=2500$, $K=2$, $Q=30$ ICE iterations, margins known (their parameters are
not re-estimated, whereas §4.3 of the paper re-estimates them). Two designs, each with the original prior $\porig$ and a
balanced prior $\pbal$ ($p_{ii}=0.35$, $p_{12}=p_{21}=0.15$):
\begin{itemize}
  \item exp.~1: candidates $\{c^1,c^3,c^6\}$ (Gauss, Gumbel, Clayton), true
        copulas $c_{11}=c^1$ ($\tau=0.7$), $c_{12}=c_{21}=c^3$ ($\tau=0.4$),
        $c_{22}=c^6$ ($\tau=0.7$), Gaussian margins;
  \item exp.~2: candidates $\{c^2,c^3,c^5,c^6\}$ (Student, Gumbel, cubic
        section, Clayton), true copulas $c_{11}=c^2$ ($\tau=0.25$),
        $c_{12}=c_{21}=c^5$ ($\tau=0.10$), $c_{22}=c^6$ ($\tau=0.20$),
        Gamma margins.
\end{itemize}
At every M-step, ICE re-selects the family of each copula among the
candidates and estimates its~$\tau$. The package's ICE estimates copulas by
weighted pseudo-likelihood under the pair posteriors $\xi_n(i,j)$; the paper
(§4.2, Eqs.~22--24) estimates them on the hard sub-samples of a single
posterior draw ($L=1$). The two coincide only when the posterior is
concentrated. The paper selects the families with the Bayesian criterion of
Huard, \'Evin and Favre~\cite{huard2006} (its Eq.~20); we report that
criterion here and compare six criteria in Section~\ref{sec:exp4}.

\subsection{A single ICE start ends in the wrong basin}\label{sec:exp3-basin}

The first reproduction started ICE from every copula on the first candidate
with $\tau=0$. With Gaussian margins it rarely recovers the diagonal
copulas: $c_{11}$ in $2\,\%$ of runs with $\porig$, $c_{11}$ and $c_{22}$ in
$0\,\%$ with $\pbal$ (Table~\ref{tab:exp3}). Three checks locate the cause in
the convergence of ICE rather than in the selection criterion:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{With the true parameters}, the M-step (MLE criterion) selects
        the true diagonal families in 10 seeds out of 10 in all four
        settings, whether it is fed the true labels, the posterior $\xi$ or
        one posterior draw.
  \item \emph{Started at the true model}, ICE (MLE criterion, exp.~1) keeps
        the true families (diagonal 10/10) and ends with a log-likelihood
        higher than the single start by a median of $30$~nat ($\porig$) and
        $58$~nat ($\pbal$) over 10 seeds. Its unsupervised error with $\porig$ is
        $13.7\,\%$, the paper's $13.74\,\%$.
  \item \emph{The k-means warm start of the package} was no remedy: it
        numbered the states arbitrarily and, with known margins, estimated
        the prior and copulas on permuted labels in about half the starts.
        This defect is fixed (clusters are now matched to the declared state
        laws).
\end{enumerate}
The family of a start decides the basin. The package's own multistart, which
only perturbs the parameters of a start, keeps its families and cannot leave
that basin.

\subsection{Multistart ICE}

The multistart runs ICE from the single start above, from the corrected
k-means start, and from every combination of candidate families on the two
diagonal copulas ($\tau=0.5$; off-diagonal copulas on the first candidate,
$\tau=0.3$), and keeps the run of highest final log-likelihood. No start uses
the truth. Table~\ref{tab:exp3} compares it with the single start and with
the paper.

  \label{tab:exp3}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Diagonal copulas are recovered} in $97$--$100\,\%$ of runs in
        all four settings (paper: $80$--$100\,\%$). With Gaussian margins the
        winning start is a diagonal sweep in every run (Huard criterion).
  \item \textbf{The unsupervised error comes within $0.5$~point of the
        supervised one} ($14.7$ vs $14.4\,\%$, $10.3$ vs $10.2\,\%$, $20.9$ vs
        $20.4\,\%$, $20.7$ vs $20.4\,\%$): estimation costs almost nothing once
        ICE starts in the right basin. From a single start, the loss with
        Gaussian margins was $3.4$ and $10.8$ points.
  \item \textbf{Off-diagonal copulas stay mostly below the paper}:
        $43$--$83\,\%$ against $70$--$100\,\%$ (only $c_{21}$ of exp.~1,

        and parameters, the cubic-section copula of exp.~2, $\porig$, is

        weighted Vuong tests~\cite{vuong1989} declared these choices
        statistically tied with the runner-up in $75$--$100\,\%$ of the runs,
        the true family always belonging to the set of families not
        significantly worse than the selected one. The paper's
        9--10 out of 10 rest on ten runs.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{What remains}\label{sec:exp3-remaining}

With Gaussian margins and $\porig$, the \emph{supervised} error of our
simulations is $14.4\,\%$ against $12.16\,\%$ in the paper. This excess does
not involve ICE and has the same sign as the bias of Table~3(a); with Gamma
margins our supervised error is lower than the paper's ($10.2$ vs
$11.35\,\%$). Neither is explained by the hypotheses of
Section~\ref{sec:agreement}.

\section{Experiment~4 --- Comparison of selection criteria}\label{sec:exp4}

\subsection{Criteria}

\begin{description}
  \item[MLE] $\arg\max_r \log L_r(\hat\tau_r)$, the weighted log-likelihood
    at the fitted parameter.
  \item[AIC] $\arg\max_r 2\log L_r - 2k_r$.
  \item[BIC] $\arg\max_r 2\log L_r - k_r\log n_{\text{eff}}$, with
    $n_{\text{eff}}=\sum_n\xi_n(i,j)$.
  \item[Huard] $\arg\max_r \frac{1}{\tau_M^r-\tau_m^r}
    \int_{\tau_m^r}^{\tau_M^r}\!\prod_n c_r(u_n,v_n;\tau)^{\xi_n}\,d\tau$
    (paper, Eq.~20): Bayesian evidence under a uniform prior on each
    family's admissible $\tau$-range, computed by adaptive quadrature.
  \item[CvM] $\arg\min_r S_n(c_r)$, the weighted Cram\'er--von Mises distance
    between empirical and fitted copula CDFs.
  \item[xv-CIC] cross-validated copula information criterion: out-of-sample
    log-likelihood on contiguous, purged folds.
\end{description}

\subsection{With pair margins and multistart}

Table~\ref{tab:exp4-multi} summarises the six criteria on the reproduction of
Section~\ref{sec:exp3} ($D$: diagonal recovery, $O$: off-diagonal recovery,
$\bar e$: unsupervised error; 30 runs).

{\setlength{\tabcolsep}{2.2pt}\input{tables/synthesis/exp4_criterion_summary_multistart.tex}}

MLE, AIC, BIC, Huard and xv-CIC all recover the diagonal copulas in
$97$--$100\,\%$ of runs and reach the same error to within $0.1$~point. They
differ off-diagonal only, where Huard is the best likelihood-based criterion
in every setting ($78$, $57$, $53$ and $70\,\%$ against $53$, $35$, $45$ and
$53\,\%$ for MLE). CvM is the only criterion that fails on the diagonal
($35$--$85\,\%$) and it has the highest error with Gaussian margins.

\subsection{The earlier benchmark: one margin per state, single start}

The rest of this section analyses the earlier design --- one margin per state
and a single ICE start, 100 runs --- in which the criteria were first compared
and in which the prior-width effect behind Huard's off-diagonal advantage
was isolated. That design is not the paper's model and its error rates are
inflated by the wrong ICE basin, but it is a controlled benchmark for
comparing criteria on identical data, and its conclusions on the criteria
stand. Its summary is Table~\ref{tab:exp4-summary}; each rate pools 200
Bernoulli trials and the runs are paired across criteria (run~$r$ uses seed
$3000+r$), so comparisons use McNemar's exact test on per-run outcomes.

{\setlength{\tabcolsep}{2.2pt}\input{tables/exp4_criterion_summary.tex}}

\paragraph{(1) The likelihood-based criteria agree with one another.} MLE,
AIC and BIC return identical recovery rates on both exp.~1 settings and are
within $1.5$~points on exp.~2, $\porig$. They part company on exp.~2, $\pbal$,
where AIC reaches $O=16\,\%$ against MLE's $8\,\%$ (McNemar $20{:}5$,
$p=0.004$) and BIC $D=98\,\%$ against $90\,\%$ ($16{:}0$,
$p=3.1\times10^{-5}$). At these sample sizes the penalties only arbitrate
near-ties. Every one of them recovers the two-parameter Student copula of
exp.~2 in $100\,\%$ of runs.

\paragraph{(2) Huard dominates off-diagonal.} Huard matches MLE on the
diagonal ($56.5/98/50/91\,\%$ against $57/97/50/90\,\%$), although BIC and
AIC do better there on exp.~2, $\pbal$ ($98$ and $94.5\,\%$; BIC against
Huard $14{:}0$, $p=1.2\times10^{-4}$). Off-diagonal, Huard is higher than MLE
in all four settings: $35$ vs $27\,\%$ (exp.~1, $\porig$), $41$ vs $7\,\%$
(exp.~2, $\porig$), $17.5$ vs $11.5\,\%$ (exp.~1, $\pbal$) and $38.5$ vs
$8\,\%$ (exp.~2, $\pbal$). All four gaps are significant
against MLE: $19{:}3$ ($p=8.6\times10^{-4}$), $68{:}0$
($p=6.8\times10^{-21}$), $12{:}0$ ($p=4.9\times10^{-4}$) and $61{:}0$
($p=8.7\times10^{-19}$).

Sparsity alone does not explain it. Both experiments share the off-diagonal
sparsity of $\porig$, but the size of Huard's edge depends on the true
family (Table~\ref{tab:huard-cells}): $+4$ to $+12$~points when it is Gumbel,
whose $\tau$-range $[0,1]$ has width~1, and $+30$ to $+36$~points when it is
the cubic-section copula, whose range $[0,0.165]$ is six times narrower.
Eq.~(20) places a uniform prior on each family's own range, so a narrow range
concentrates prior mass.

  \centering\small

           and McNemar discordance (Huard only : MLE only).}
  \label{tab:huard-cells}

\end{table}

\emph{An isolation experiment settles it.} The \texttt{huard\_common}
criterion is the same evidence integrated over one \emph{shared} support ---
the intersection of the candidates' declared ranges --- so that no family is
rewarded for declaring a narrow one. Same data, truth, candidates and seeds;
only the prior support changes (Table~\ref{tab:prior-width}).

\begin{table}[htbp]
  \centering\small
  \caption{Off-diagonal recovery with Huard's own supports and with a common
           support. ``Edge retained'': share of Huard's advantage over MLE
           that survives the common support. Discordance: Huard only : common
           only.}
  \label{tab:prior-width}
  \setlength{\tabcolsep}{3.5pt}
  \input{tables/synthesis/prior_width_isolation_state.tex}
\end{table}

With a common support, Huard's advantage disappears in three settings (a
retained share of $3$, $0$ and $-3\,\%$) and $38\,\%$ of it --- $+3.0$
points, $p=0.03$ --- survives in the fourth. \textbf{Huard's off-diagonal
superiority in this benchmark is almost entirely an effect of the families'
declared $\tau$-ranges, not of averaging the likelihood over~$\tau$.} It is
genuine Bayesian behaviour --- a narrower prior that contains the truth
\emph{ought} to win --- but it is a property of the candidate set, and it will
not transfer to candidate sets whose ranges are comparable. A methodological
note developing this point is in preparation.

\paragraph{(3) The prior of the chain matters more than the criterion.}
Within a setting, the unsupervised errors of the six criteria span about
$1$ to $3$~points; between settings they range from $22.0\,\%$ (exp.~2,
$\porig$, BIC) to $33.5\,\%$ (exp.~2, $\pbal$, CvM). Going from $\porig$ to
$\pbal$ raises the error by $1.6$ to $10.6$ points depending on the criterion and the
design: balanced priors switch states more often and weaken the Markov
contrast the classifier relies on.

\paragraph{(4) Cram\'er--von Mises trades diagonal for off-diagonal.} CvM
has the lowest error on both exp.~1 settings ($27.1\,\%$ and $28.8\,\%$) and
the highest off-diagonal recovery of the benchmark ($O=48\,\%$ on exp.~1,
$\pbal$, beating Huard by $74$ cells to $13$, $p=1.6\times10^{-11}$), but its
diagonal recovery collapses to $14$ and $17\,\%$ on exp.~2, where it almost
never selects the Student copula. The $L^2$ distance weights every deviation
equally, including where the density is small: a liability when two families
differ in the tails, an asset on sparse blocks where a likelihood overfits.
The multistart reproduction confirms its diagonal weakness
(Table~\ref{tab:exp4-multi}).

\paragraph{(5) xv-CIC: better founded, not better performing.} It tracks
AIC and BIC on the diagonal ($58/97/50/94\,\%$) and lies between MLE and
Huard off-diagonal in three settings, but it is the worst of the six
off-diagonal on exp.~2, $\porig$ ($O=4\,\%$), where Huard beats it by $74$
cells to~$0$ ($p=1.1\times10^{-22}$). Cross-validation lets the effective
penalty adapt to the family, which suits plug-in margins and posterior
weights better than a fixed penalty; at these sample sizes the fixed penalty
is good enough, and xv-CIC cost about $5.6$ times an MLE evaluation per block
on a synthetic benchmark.

\paragraph{On statistical power.} The conclusions on Huard rest on
discordances as one-sided as $68{:}0$, $61{:}0$ and $12{:}0$. Two
comparisons are inconclusive and reported as such: Huard vs CvM off-diagonal
on exp.~2 ($p=0.52$ and $p=0.66$) and Huard vs xv-CIC on exp.~1, $\porig$
($p=0.22$).

\paragraph{Practical recommendation.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Start ICE several times}, varying the copula families of the
        starts; the criterion matters far less than the basin.
  \item \textbf{For copula identification, Huard} is as good as MLE on the
        diagonal and better off-diagonal, but its edge comes from the declared
        $\tau$-ranges. Use it when a candidate's plausible range is genuinely
        narrow; do not expect it when the ranges are comparable.
  \item \textbf{MLE, AIC and BIC} are sound defaults when the goal is
        classification; they agree except on near-ties.
  \item \textbf{CvM} is a specialist for suspect likelihoods or rare
        transitions, never a default: it cannot identify the Student copula.
  \item \textbf{xv-CIC} is a diagnostic rather than a default.
  \item Off-diagonal choices on a hundred observations are often
        statistical ties; test them (Vuong) before interpreting them.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{The model matters first.} With pair-indexed margins --- the
        general PMC of the paper --- and the variance reading of Table~1,
        four of the eight supervised tables are reproduced to within half a
        point on average; with one margin per state, which is a hidden Markov
        chain, every table was 7 to 18 points off on average.
  \item \textbf{The qualitative conclusions of the paper hold.} The true
        copula is the best restoration copula (27 columns out of 28); the copula shape matters at
        $\tau=0.70$, where a wrong copula can multiply the error by $3.5$ to
        $5.7$; Markovianity lowers the error by $32$--$42\,\%$ at
        $\tau=0.16$.
  \item \textbf{ICE with automatic copula selection works once it starts in
        the right basin.} A multistart over the diagonal copula families
        recovers the diagonal copulas in $97$--$100\,\%$ of runs and loses at
        most about half a point of error with respect to the supervised
        restoration. Off-diagonal copulas estimated on about a hundred
        observations are often statistically undecidable.
  \item \textbf{Open gaps}: Table~3(a) and the supervised error with Gaussian
        margins (about $+2$ to $+5$ points), unexplained by seven tested
        hypotheses on the margins and the simulator, and the PMM
        Tables~4(b) and~5(b).
\end{enumerate}
